Why this exists

Every tender has a paper trail. This stops it getting lost.

Bidding for and running a project at Algihaz means producing dozens of specific documents — compliance certificates, quality plans, risk registers, performance reports — each with its own deadline and its own responsible person. Before this platform, tracking that fell to spreadsheets and memory.

The platform turns that paper trail into one live checklist per tender: what's due, who owns it, whether it's late, and who needs to sign off. Nothing on the list disappears into an inbox.

Two phases, one record

L0 is the bid. L1 is the job you won.

Every tender starts as an L0 — the bidding phase, before Algihaz has won anything. If the bid is awarded, that same tender becomes an L1: the actual project, now under contract. It isn't a new record with a new number; it's the same tender one stage further along, so its history carries forward.

L0 · Bidding

Tender

Runs from the announcement to the bid submission date (BSD). Deliverables here are compliance docs, pricing, technical proposals — tight deadlines, often measured in days.

→
L1 · Awarded

Project

Starts once the contract is signed. Deliverables shift to execution: quality governance, reporting cycles, project closeout — deadlines measured in weeks and months.

Both stages share the same key dates (announcement, site visit, pre-bid meeting) — L1's deadlines are calculated from the same anchor points L0 used, so the schedule never has to be re-entered by hand.

The checklist itself

A standing catalog of what's actually required

Rather than someone deciding fresh each time what documents a tender needs, there's a pre-built catalog — maintained by an Admin — of every deliverable type Algihaz might need to produce, organized by stage.

Who does what

Three roles, three jobs

Every person acting in the platform is one of three roles for the deliverables they're involved in:

Sees everything Admin

Full run of the platform — creates tenders/projects, manages the deliverable catalog, assigns Owners and SMEs, and can act on any item regardless of who it's assigned to.

No restriction — the one role that bypasses every assignment check.

Does the work Owner

The person responsible for actually producing a deliverable — uploads the document, marks it complete, and resubmits if it's sent back.

Can only act on deliverables they're personally assigned as Owner on.

Signs off SME

Subject Matter Expert — reviews what the Owner submitted and approves or rejects it. A deliverable can have more than one SME; any one of them can act.

Can only review deliverables they're personally assigned as SME on — and can't approve their own work.

A deliverable can list several Owners or several SMEs — any one of them acting is enough, which matters when the responsible person is out.

Who can see what Around 95% of a tender is visible to everyone in the platform by design — that's the whole point, sharing information instead of it living in one person's inbox. A handful of genuinely sensitive fields (the bid value, for one) are restricted to specific people, and anyone else can request access, which an Admin approves or rejects.
Life of one deliverable

If an SME sends work back, it returns to In progress as Rejected rather than disappearing — the Owner resubmits and it re-enters review. This status (how far along the work is) is tracked separately from the deadline (whether it's on time) — a deliverable can be fully approved and still have landed late, and the platform records both facts rather than collapsing them into one.

No one types in a due date

Deadlines calculate themselves

Each catalog item carries its own formula — so many days before the bid submission date, so many days after the announcement, or chained off another deliverable's own date. Enter the tender's key dates once, and every one of its ~hundred deliverable deadlines resolves on its own.

A handful of milestones (M1–M6) aren't separate checkpoints — a milestone simply is a specific catalog item; reaching it means that item was approved. On L1 specifically, milestones don't just track progress, they unlock it: only around 10% of deliverables are open at the start, and reaching each milestone opens the next batch — roughly half of all L1 deliverables wait on just one of them, the early project plan. And if something upstream runs late, everything chained off it slides forward automatically rather than quietly going overdue against a date that's no longer realistic.

Seeing the whole picture

Dashboards, performance, and a running score

Beyond the per-tender checklist, the platform rolls everything up: how many L0s and L1s are active, how many items are overdue right now, and how each department is performing against its own deliverables.

Once something is approved, it earns a score from 1.1 (early) down to 0.0 (very late) — on-time submissions get a small grace window rather than being penalized for landing on the deadline itself. That feeds a department and individual performance ranking, so "who's actually keeping up" is a number, not an impression.

The rest of the system
  • NotificationsIn-app announcements fire on every meaningful event — assigned to you, sent for review, approaching due, overdue — and can go out as real email once the organization's Outlook is connected. Not assigned to something but still want updates? Follow it, and you'll get notified anyway.
  • GAHIZA built-in AI assistant Owners and SMEs can ask questions — "what's still open on this tender," "why is this item due when it is" — answered from the platform's own data, not a generic chatbot.
  • FilesEvery upload can live in Algihaz's own OneDrive once connected, instead of the platform's own storage — the same document trail the rest of the business already uses.
  • EscalationAn overdue item doesn't just sit there — reminders step up automatically at 2, 7, and 14 days late if no one's acted.
Honest about where this is today

Still a pilot in one specific way

There's no real login yet. Who you're acting as is a self-reported email typed into the app (or, inside the Algihaz SSO container, filled in automatically) — the three roles above are enforced by checking that email against the assignment list, not by a verified identity. That's adequate for a pilot; before this goes company-wide, the same checks need enforcing with real authentication behind them, not just the honor system this runs on today.
